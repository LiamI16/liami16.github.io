## The problem

I tried to read a paper on a long flight and realized I hadn't downloaded any music —
the cache I had was three albums already looped into the ground. Pre-downloading for
trips works in theory, but storage fills up and even good albums go stale over a long
enough session. I wanted something that loads once, plays forever, and never repeats
enough for my brain to pattern-match it the way a track on a loop eventually does. That
became Loam: a seed goes in, an infinite, reproducible, offline lo-fi soundscape comes
out — a few hundred kilobytes, entirely client-side, no accounts or streaming.

## How it works

### A Minecraft-style seed, not a playlist

Loam doesn't store or stream audio — it generates it. A 64-bit seed deterministically
drives every random decision the engine makes, the same way a Minecraft seed determines
a whole world. Same seed in, byte-for-byte-identical *event sequence* out, on any
browser or platform, forever. That determinism is what makes a seed shareable as a
plain number and pinnable as a favorite.

Underneath, the seed is never used directly. It's expanded with **PCG32** (a compact,
statistically strong PRNG — chosen over `Math.random()`, which isn't seedable or
reproducible across browsers) and every subsystem asks for its own *named child seed*
via `splitmix64(root, hash("melody/density-fbm"))`, `splitmix64(root, hash("harmony/markov"))`,
and so on. That namespacing is what lets the engine add a whole new subsystem — an
ornament layer, a new fBm stream — without perturbing the RNG sequence every other
subsystem already depends on. Two consumers never accidentally share a stream, so
existing seeds don't silently change character when new features ship.

The real design problem is subtler than "make music from a seed" — it's making two
seeds feel like different *places* without either one ever feeling like a labeled
category ("the jazzy seed," "the dense seed"). The engine's identity model is a
five-layer stack: universal 1/f (fBm) drift on every parameter; per-seed *shape* on
that drift (a different mean, clamp range, and timescale per seed); a handful of
per-seed *couplings* between otherwise-independent streams; a fixed per-seed
orchestration/mix bias (the actual anti-ergodic backbone — a constant "who's playing"
timbre a seed never drifts away from); and per-seed weighting over structured choices
like the chord Markov walk. No single layer is sufficient alone — pure fBm is ergodic
(every seed eventually visits the same territory given long enough), and fixed
per-seed knobs read as static over an hours-long session. Layering all five is what
produces emergent, un-labelable personality.

### Harmony: a hand-tuned Markov walk, perturbed per seed

Chords come from a sparse, hand-tuned transition matrix over 15 chords diatonic to
C major/A minor plus two borrowed colors (`Fm6`, `Bbmaj7`) — biased toward vamps like
i↔IV and workhorses like vi→IV→I, with borrowed colors reachable but rare. At
construction, each seed draws its own perturbed copy of that matrix by sampling every
row from a Dirichlet distribution centered on the hand-tuned weights (`Dir(α · p)`,
α=20 as the sweet spot between "every seed sounds identical" and "every seed sounds
like a different piece entirely") — implemented from scratch with a Marsaglia–Tsang
gamma sampler and Box–Muller normals, since it all has to come from the seeded `Rng`,
never `Math.random`. A stateful Markov walk then steps through that per-seed matrix
one chord at a time.

Voicing is a greedy nearest-pitch voice-leading solver: each previous voice moves to
the closest chord tone of the new chord, in any octave, so common tones are retained
"for free" and the harmony never jumps around motionlessly.

### Melody: a persistent germ, not a strategy machine

Melody is the layer that carries the most per-seed identity — and the hardest one to
get right, since "generate an infinite melody" easily collapses into either
random-sounding noodling or audibly looping motifs. Loam's answer, after explicitly
rejecting both a pure-activity Bernoulli model and an explicit phrase-and-strategy
architecture, is an L-system-flavored middle path: at construction, each seed generates
a small 3–5 note **germ** — its persistent melodic axiom, picked from ten hand-authored
contour templates (rising-arc, falling-stepwise, pivot-tone, cradle/rocking, bell
arpeggio, …) with seed-specific starting pitch, interval bias, and length. That germ
never decays; it's replayed verbatim, transformed (transpose, invert, retrograde,
fragment, augment/diminish), recombined from a rolling buffer of recent notes, or
occasionally set aside for a fresh chord-aware pitch — the mix of those four choices is
itself Dirichlet-perturbed per seed, so some seeds stay close to their germ and others
wander.

Whether melody plays at all is gated by a **min-cap coupling** with the chord layer's
own activity stream:

```
effective_melody_activity =
    (1 − coupling) · melody_activity
  + coupling · min(melody_activity, 1 − chord_activity)
```

which was chosen over five other candidate formulas (pure inverse blending,
multiplicative suppression, additive suppression, an exponential/Boltzmann variant, and
an elegant-but-rejected phase-coupling model on the unit circle) specifically because it
preserves each seed's own density character at *every* coupling value — a sparse seed
stays sparse even when strongly coupled, because the chord layer only crowds melody out
when it's actually busy, never uniformly suppresses it. The phase-coupling alternative
was the closest runner-up but was rejected because at high coupling it forces melody
active whenever chords are quiet, which kills the mutual-silence moments that calm lofi
depends on.

### The generation substrate: fractional Brownian motion as the "alive" primitive

Almost every knob that should feel alive rather than static — filter cutoff, chord and
melody activity, the melody/chord coupling strength itself — is driven by **fBm**
(fractional Brownian motion: stacked octaves of deterministic value noise, tuned to
roughly a 1/f spectrum, the same target Voss & Clarke identified as reading
"musically" — more structured than white noise, livelier than brown noise). Each fBm
stream exposes a uniform `ParamStream.evaluate(time)` interface, sampled at
*engine-time*, i.e. the exact moment a scheduling decision is made — not on a fixed
timer — which is what keeps the whole system reproducible from the seed alone
regardless of how the browser's event loop happens to schedule ticks. A per-seed
`depth` and base frequency (also drawn from the seed, never user-facing) determine how
wide and how fast each stream drifts, which is the mechanism behind, e.g., "some seeds
are consistently sparse, others build and recede over minutes."

### Core/adapter split

`@loam/core` is pure generation logic with zero audio dependency: it emits a typed
stream of `NoteEvent` / `ParamEvent` / `TickEvent`s on an engine-time clock. A thin
Tone.js adapter (`@loam/synth-tone`) is the only layer that touches Web Audio, resolving
string channel names (`rhodes`, `pad`, `kick`, …) and dotted parameter paths
(`fx.evoFilter.cutoff`) to actual synth nodes. That boundary is what makes the core
unit-testable in Node with `vitest`, portable if Tone.js is ever swapped out, and the
reason the whole engine can be pinned with locked-sequence determinism tests — a
known seed produces a known byte-identical event sequence, checked layer by layer
(PRNG → value noise → fBm → Markov walk → whole-engine fingerprint), so a regression in
any one layer fails a specific, narrow test instead of a vague "the music sounds off."
Scheduling itself follows the standard Web Audio lookahead pattern: the engine
generates events 100–200ms ahead of when they fire and hands them to the adapter with
an exact engine-time, so a GC pause or a busy JS thread never causes an audible glitch —
already-scheduled audio plays on time regardless.

## Where it stands

The core engine and hosted web demo are shipped; an Obsidian plugin (same engine,
playing inside notes) is in progress. Deliberately out of scope for v1: sample
libraries, ML-driven generation, cloud sync, accounts — the generative,
kilobytes-not-gigabytes identity is the point. Full design rationale, including every
rejected alternative and why, lives in [`docs/`](https://github.com/LiamI16/loam/tree/main/docs)
in the repo.
